Abstract

Humanoid robots are increasingly expected to operate in real-world scenarios requiring heavy-duty whole-body transportation, including logistics, construction, and industrial material handling. However, transporting large and heavy objects fundamentally changes humanoid control dynamics: strong coupling between the robot and payload induces severe center-of-mass shifts, momentum amplification, and destabilizing whole-body oscillations that frequently break existing balance. In this work, we introduce Unwieldy-Bench, a stress-test benchmark for humanoid transportation under heavy-load and inertially challenging object dynamics. Our analysis shows that representative imitation-based controllers degrade sharply in this regime because conventional approaches primarily optimize pose and trajectory tracking while largely ignoring whole-body momentum evolution. Motivated by this insight, we introduce a lightweight momentum-aware shaping strategy to regularize payload-induced angular momentum. Experiments demonstrate that even this simple modification improves transportation stability, reduces object drop rates, and extends the feasible load range across diverse unwieldy transportation scenarios.
